SLA Target Boundaries: p95 Latency <= 500ms, Error Rate <= 1.0%
Date: 2026-09-05
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. BASELINE (Average-Based Capacity Planning)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Planning Philosophy: Sized based on average historical traffic (~1,300 RPM).
- Configuration: 4 initial instances, hard ceiling of 6 instances (3,000 RPM capacity).
- Measured Results under DISASTER_PEAK:
  * Peak Demand:            5,027 RPM
  * Peak Available Capacity: 3,000 RPM (Capacity Gap: 2,027 RPM)
  * Peak Active Instances:   6 instances (Capped at maximum ceiling)
  * Maximum Queue Depth:    50,000 queued requests
  * Maximum p95 Latency:    62,630 ms (SLA Target: 500ms -> BREACHED)
  * Maximum Error Rate:     65.29% (HTTP 503 drops)
  * Total Unmet Requests:   350,416 dropped user requests
  * SLA Compliance:         20.6% (SLA Status: FAILED)


2. TARGET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Objective: Maintain 100% SLA compliance (p95 latency <= 500ms, error rate <= 1.0%)
  even when subjected to a 4.0x disaster traffic surge. Zero dropped requests.


3. MEASURED RESULT (Scenario-Based Capacity Planning)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Planning Philosophy: Sized explicitly to absorb disaster peak demand curve.
- Configuration: 10 initial instances, maximum ceiling of 20 instances (10,000 RPM capacity).
- Measured Results under DISASTER_PEAK:
  * Peak Demand:            5,027 RPM
  * Peak Available Capacity: 7,000 RPM (Sufficient Headroom: +1,973 RPM)
  * Peak Active Instances:   14 instances
  * Maximum Queue Depth:    0 queued requests (Zero queue buildup)
  * Maximum p95 Latency:    384 ms (SLA Target: 500ms -> PASSED)
  * Maximum Error Rate:     0.18%
  * Total Unmet Requests:   0 dropped requests
  * SLA Compliance:         100.0% (SLA Status: PASSED)


4. ERROR ANALYSIS & ROOT CAUSE EXPLANATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why does Average-Based Planning Fail?
Average-based planning relies on steady-state mean utilization (~1,300 RPM) and sets
an instance ceiling of 6 servers (3,000 RPM max). When a disaster strikes:
1. Demand surges to 5,027 RPM within 15 minutes.
2. The infrastructure ceiling of 3,000 RPM creates an unbridgeable 2,200 RPM shortfall.
3. Queue depth rapidly expands to 50,000 backlog items.
4. M/M/1 queuing delay causes p95 latency to explode to 62,630ms.
5. The queue buffer overflows, dropping 350,416 critical emergency user requests.

Why does Scenario-Based Planning Succeed?
Scenario-based planning models the non-uniform disaster surge curve in advance. By expanding the
instance ceiling to 20 servers and starting with 10 warm instances, auto-scaling successfully
scales to 14 instances, matching demand in real-time and maintaining 100% SLA compliance.


5. RECOMMENDATION & CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMENDATION: Transition immediately from Average-Based to Scenario-Based Capacity Planning.

Conclusion:
Scenario-based planning directly improves service-level compliance from 20.6% to 100.0%,
completely eliminating the 350,416 dropped user requests during disaster emergencies.

Infrastructure Sizing Recommendation for DISASTER_PEAK:
- Base Provisioning: 10 active instances (5,000 RPM baseline buffer)
- Maximum Ceiling:  20 active instances (10,000 RPM max ceiling)
- Scaling Policy:   Scale out at 70% CPU/RPM utilization with 180s scaling propagation delay.
